# Critic review: b4 (N THE NIGHT, B PUTTING IT BACK), `web/shots_b4.js`

**Verdict: FIX REQUIRED (close, but not shippable yet).** This is the strongest chapter set so far: the night clock runs forward correctly (7:20 P.M. → 4:05 A.M.), the seal goes intact → cut → forged, "They cut it." lands, and the blackout eye-whites gag and the socks insert are keepers. The problems:
- Three beats miss their word: no flash under the tarp on "flashes", the yard is still lit on "dark", and no ladder on "climbed".
- The imagined ambush asserts *Soviet* guards.
- Several frames are empty or illegible: the plastic/stamp/wire void, the couplings that look like jars, cropped title boxes, and a ghost label.

Method: contact sheets `contact/critic_b4_01..08.jpg`, plus snaps at 511.6, 514.5, 515.9, 520.0, 540.6, 573.4, 629.4 and 638.0 s. Word times come from `voice/lines.json`.

## Required fixes

1. **MAJOR: N8.1/0 (imagined ambush, truth rule).**
   - **Problem:** at 519–520 s, five guards in ushankas with red stars rise over a red fence. The script only says "it looked like an ambush". It never says by whom. Drawing Soviet uniforms asserts who they feared. There is also no on-screen signal that this is imagined, beyond the red tint.
   - **Change:** make them faceless black silhouettes with no insignia and no Soviet hats (generic hat/shoulder shapes), with a few eye glints at most. Add a small tag "WHAT IT LOOKED LIKE" (or "FOR A FEW SECONDS…"). Keep the red grade and the heavy grain. Then use a hard cut back to the real, empty fence on "But no one came" (N9.0), which already works.

2. **MAJOR: N12.1/0–1 ("so the camera flashes wouldn't light up the sky").**
   - **Problem:** at 540.6 s, just after "flashes" (540.24), the tarp-over-crate exterior shows no flash at all. The picture never shows the flashes it is about.
   - **Change:**
     - On 'flashes', pulse a white-blue glow *under* the tarp three times (0.12 s each, 0.5 s apart) that lights only the tarp's underside and the crate top. The sky above must stay dark.
     - Add `camflash2` sfx at low gain (-18) for each pulse.
     - Optionally, on "sky", add a tiny tilt up to the still-dark sky (it already tilts).

3. **MAJOR: B6.1/0 ("Around four in the morning, the yard went dark").**
   - **Problem:** at 628.5 s the yard is still fully lit. The lights must die on "dark" (628.98). Right now the blackout only arrives in the next shot, B7.0, so the word lands on a lit frame.
   - **Change:** in B6.1, kill all drop lights and street lamps on `T.cue('dark')`: a 2-frame flicker, then black, leaving only eye-whites, like B7.0. Fire `switch` + `elec` sfx on 'dark'. B7.0 then continues in darkness.

4. **MAJOR: N6.0/0 ("Then they climbed onto the roof").**
   - **Problem:** the left specialist hovers beside the crate wall with no ladder; he is floating. The kit has ladders, and the team bought them in M7 (a continuity payoff).
   - **Change:** lean a ladder against the crate's left side. The specialist is on it mid-climb (pose `climb`) and reaches the roof on "roof". The tool sheet at bottom-right is cropped by the frame edge: move it fully inside, or drop it.

5. **MAJOR: N21.1/0–1 ("plastic, the stamp, and the wire").**
   - **Problem:** at 586 s and 588 s the frame is a brown void with one small red square labelled PLASTIC for about 2 s, then three small objects. It is empty and dull for the payoff of "we can forge it".
   - **Change:**
     - Stage it on the embassy desk under the green lamp (set continuity with N21.0), shot as a top-down or 3/4 insert, filling the frame.
     - Land the three items one per word ('plastic', 'stamp', 'wire'), each larger, around 25% of frame height.
     - On 'wire', the stamp presses into the warm plastic: show a 'stamp' sfx and a small CCCP impression.

6. **MAJOR: N22.0/0–1 and N22.1 (the couplings).**
   - **Problem:** the "couplings" read as jars or lanterns (rounded cans with lids). Nobody watching will know what they are. They also float in a black void with no rocket around them.
   - **Change:**
     - Draw them as pipe couplings: short metal sleeves with hex nut ends and threaded rims, grey/brass.
     - Put them inside the Lunik's lit interior (the tank wall from N16 in the background), where the torch finds them.
     - Keep the "TWO COUPLINGS" label, and add the tag "FORGOTTEN TO REMOVE" there rather than in N22.1.

7. **MAJOR: N19.1/1 (flash-forward, "the kind of thing a checker would notice").**
   - **Problem:** at 580.3 s the "THE CHECKER WOULD NOTICE" label is ghosted and cut off by the left frame edge. It is illegible.
   - **Change:**
     - Move the label to x ≥ 120 at full opacity, or drop it; the narrator already says it.
     - Add a small tag "WHAT COULD HAPPEN" (or "IF HE LOOKED") so the sepia scene is unmistakably hypothetical.
     - Give the checker his clipboard so he matches `cast_sheet.png`.

8. **MAJOR: N14.1/0 ("…at the U.S. Embassy, and it came back good").**
   - **Problem:** the tag reads "U.S. EMBASSY DARKROOM" but the scene is the salvage yard (fence, cars, corner clock SALVAGE YARD). It is mislabelled. "Came back good" also has no visual payoff: the print is shown, but nothing says *good*.
   - **Change:**
     - Change the tag to "TEST ROLL: BACK FROM THE EMBASSY".
     - On 'good', stamp a green check or "GOOD" on the print with a ding.
     - Have Finer give a small nod or grin on 'good'.

9. **MINOR: N18.0/0, N18.0/1, B1.1/1 (cropped title boxes).** "PAYLOAD I…", "PAYLOAD MOUNT" and "REASSEMBL…" are cut by the right frame edge. Pull them at least 60 px inside, or remove them; they add nothing legible.

10. **MINOR: N3.0/0 (big clock).** The big face says ABOUT 7:30 P.M. while the corner clock reads 7:23 P.M. in the same frame. Hide the corner clock during N3.0, or set it to 7:30.

11. **MINOR: N5.0/1 (WAIT sign).** The "WAIT" tag floats in the sky above the fence. Pin it to something (a hand-held note), or drop it and let the 30-MINUTES clock in N5.1 carry the beat.

12. **MINOR: N21.2/0 (duplicate seal delivery).** At 590.0 s the "DUPLICATE SEAL" label is semi-transparent and overlaps Dean's hat and the envelope. Delay the label until the envelope has landed, at full opacity and clear of heads.

13. **MINOR: N22.1/0 (arm through the clock).** A long arm/stick enters from the top-right corner and passes under the corner-clock overlay. Bring the hand in from the right side below the clock (y > 200), or drop it.

14. **MINOR: B4.0/1 (truth wording).** "NO VISIBLE DIFFERENCE" asserts something the script doesn't say; it says only that the forged seal was fitted. Use "FORGED COPY: FITTED".

15. **MINOR: B8.1/0 ("the specialists left").** The four specialists stand in a row facing camera on "left". Have them walk out through the yard gate (screen right → off) from 'left' (636.96), with `steps` sfx and the gate closing. It should feel like an ending.

16. **MINOR: N17.0/0 ("Then they ran into a problem").** It is too dark to read on a phone, and Finer is half cut by the bottom-right corner. Lift the torch pool so the seal/rod is clearly the subject, frame Finer from mid-chest up fully inside the frame, and give him the `worried` face on 'problem'. The orchestrator's music drop goes here.

17. **MINOR: N10.0/0 ("lowered themselves into the nose").** The two tiny yellow figures on ropes are about 30 px tall and unreadable at phone size, and the "CRATE + LUNIK / SECTION, LOOKING IN" box is illegible. Push in on the nose half of the blueprint (cam focus on the nose, zoom ≥ 1.6) so the lowered figures read, and drop the box.
